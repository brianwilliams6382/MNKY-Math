Tools are where MNKY Math becomes usable.

They help you move from recognizing a pattern to examining it more deliberately within a situation, conversation, decision, system, design problem, or leadership question.

A Tool may help you:

- define what you are examining
- structure where and how you look
- gather observations and evidence
- make hidden relationships easier to see
- preserve uncertainty
- improve the questions being asked
- support clearer judgment about what to do next

MNKY Math Tools are not designed to manufacture certainty.

They do not assume that every system can be reduced to a score, that every observation has one interpretation, or that completing a worksheet produces an answer.

The Tool structures attention.

The people using it still have to observe, interpret, discuss, decide, and participate.

> A Tool does not replace judgment.
>
> It gives judgment something more visible to work with.

---

## Using MNKY Math Tools

Tools can be used individually or with others.

Some offer a quick diagnostic that can be applied through a few questions. Others include stages, working maps, guides, scorecards, or examples that support a deeper examination.

A Tool may therefore contain several connected pages:

- an overview of the Tool and when to use it
- a diagnostic, guide, or scorecard
- a printable working map or worksheet
- a worked example showing the Tool in practice
- companion Tools and supporting MNKY Math context

You do not have to complete every layer for a Tool to be useful.

Begin with the part that fits the question, time, evidence, and participation available to you.

Treat the result as part of an investigation rather than a final verdict.

---

## Types of Tools

> [!info]- Diagnostics
> Diagnostics help examine a system, situation, pattern, behavior, outcome, or condition more clearly.
>
> A diagnostic may use questions, prompts, checklists, decision paths, maps, or interpretive steps.
>
> The purpose is not always to produce a score.
>
> The purpose is to reveal what may be happening, what remains unclear, and where closer attention may be useful.

> [!info]- Guides
> Guides help you move through a practice, process, or application of MNKY Math.
>
> A guide may include steps, prompts, examples, worksheets, planning questions, facilitation notes, or templates.
>
> Guides are useful when you are not only trying to understand an idea, but trying to use it.
>
> Because use is one of the ways understanding becomes more durable.

> [!info]- Scorecards
> Scorecards help examine something across multiple dimensions.
>
> A scorecard may be used with a system, practice, decision, outcome, meeting, metric, Tool, or organizational pattern.
>
> In MNKY Math, the score is never treated as the whole truth.
>
> A good scorecard structures attention. It helps people see where something may be strong, fragile, distorted, incomplete, or misaligned.

MNKY Math is a living environment.

Many Tools begin as seeds and mature through use — as they are tested against real systems, discussed with others, and connected to new observations.

The purpose is not to preserve a Tool exactly as first written.

The purpose is to make it increasingly useful for seeing and working with systems.
