# What Do We Mean by System

You’ve heard the word before.

> **_System_**

It shows up everywhere.

- Business systems
- Education systems
- Healthcare systems
- Belief systems
- Operating systems

We use the word constantly.

But rarely stop to define it.

Because not everything structured is a system.

A checklist isn’t a system.<br>
A tool isn’t a system.<br>
A policy isn’t a system.

Even a process — on its own — isn’t quite a system.

These are components.

> A system exists when components interact in a way that produces consistent outcomes over time.

Two conditions must be present:

- **Interdependence** — elements influence one another
- **Persistence** — outcomes repeat across time

Without both, there is no system.

Only isolated activity.

And here’s the shift that matters:

<span class="mm-callout-emoji" style="--emoji: ' ';">**A system is not defined by what is designed.<br>It is defined by what is produced.<br>If outcomes differ from intent, the system — not the plan — is the source of truth.**</span>

---
## The Factory

A factory is often described as a system.

And it is — but not because machines exist.

It becomes a system through interaction:

- Materials arrive
- Workflows sequence tasks
- Machines process inputs
- People make decisions
- Constraints shape flow

What matters is not any single step.

It’s how they connect.

When those interactions stabilize, something emerges:

<span class="mm-callout-emoji" style="--emoji: ' ';">**A consistent output.**</span>

➡️ Volume<br>
➡️ Quality<br>
➡️ Defects<br>
➡️ Delays<br>

These outcomes are not random.

<span style="text-decoration: underline;">They are produced.</span>

This is the core insight behind works like “The Goal” — that bottlenecks, flow, and throughput are not isolated problems, but properties of the system itself.

And the factory doesn’t stand alone.

It sits inside larger systems:

⬆️ **Upstream** (before the factory) — sourcing, supply chains, product decisions

⬇️ **Downstream** (after the factory) — distribution, marketing, support, disposal

Each layer interacting.

Each layer producing outcomes.

> **Systems within systems, _within systems._**

---
## Not All Systems Look Like Systems

Now consider something less obvious.

👉 **==Social media.==**

It doesn’t look like a system in the traditional sense.

There are no assembly lines.<br>
No physical outputs.

But the same conditions apply.

- **Interdependence**: Algorithms, content, users, incentives, notifications — all influencing each other.
- **Persistence**: Certain behaviors repeat.

✅ Scrolling<br>
✅ Posting<br>
✅ Reacting<br>
✅ Returning<br>

And over time, the pattern becomes familiar.

- You know when something will land.
- You know when it won’t.

So behavior adjusts.

For some, that shows up in what gets posted.

- **_How_** it’s said.
- **_When_** it’s shared.

For others, it looks quieter.

- What gets watched.
- What gets skipped.
- How long something holds attention.
- When you leave — and when you come back.

No post required.

Because the system doesn’t just respond to what you say.

> **It responds to what you do.**

→ What you pause on.<br>→ What you replay.<br>→ What you scroll past without finishing.

➖ ➖ ➖

Even when you think you’re not choosing — **_the system is learning from what you don’t resist_.**

And over time, something shifts.

Not just behavior.

<span class="mm-callout-emoji" style="--emoji: ' ';">**==Preference==**</span>

What holds attention begins to define what feels interesting.

What doesn’t… disappears faster each time.

You don’t experience this as pressure.

You experience it as instinct. Your Instinct. Something that _feels_ like your own.

But the outcomes are consistent:

- More time spent.
- More return behavior.
- More alignment with what the system <span style="text-decoration: underline;">surfaces</span>.

And less of what it doesn’t.

Whether you post or not doesn’t change this.

> Because participation isn’t defined by contribution. **It’s defined by interaction.**

And over time, it becomes harder to tell where your [[def_preference|preferences]] end…

…where the system’s preferences begin,

…==and who exactly is shaping your preferences==.

> Even if it doesn’t look like a system — **if it acts like one, _it is one_.**

---
## What This Changes

Once you see systems this way, a pattern becomes clear:

☝️ If something keeps happening. <span style="text-decoration: underline;">It is being produced.</span>

Not by chance. Not by isolated decisions.

<span class="mm-callout-emoji" style="--emoji: ' ';">👍 **By a system.**</span>

And systems don’t operate at the level of individual components.

They operate across interactions.

Which is why changing a single element rarely changes the outcome — the interactions are what produce it.

To alter the outcome, the interactions must change.

Within MNKY Math, this is the working definition:

> **A system is the set of interacting elements that produces consistent patterns of behavior and outcomes over time.**

Not what is intended.

Not what is documented.

<span class="mm-callout-emoji" style="--emoji: ' ';">**What is produced.**</span>

Once you see it… you start noticing systems everywhere. 

Including the ones shaping you.

Some of which you don’t experience as systems at all, like in [[musical-chairs|musical chairs]].

Even the ones that feel like you.

---
## The simple meaning to hang with

If it keeps happening, it’s not random — **==it’s a system.==**
