# Employee Engagement

Employee engagement is supposed to be a signal.

A way to understand whether people feel connected to their work, supported by their teams, and aligned with the organization’s direction.

In theory, it’s about listening.

In practice, it often becomes something else entirely.

## When Engagement Becomes an Obligation

Most employees can tell you exactly when an engagement survey is coming.

Not because they’re eager to share their perspective, but because the reminders start piling up.

Emails.<br>Slack messages.<br>Team huddles.

“Please complete the survey.”<br>“Just a few more responses needed.”<br>“We’re almost at our target.”

At some point, the message shifts from invitation to **expectation**.

And that’s where things start to drift.

If people have to be pressured into answering a survey about how engaged they feel, the system isn’t measuring engagement anymore. It’s measuring compliance.

## When the Measure Starts Driving the Behavior

Engagement surveys are designed to reveal truth.

But when leadership fixates on response rates, something subtle happens: the metric starts shaping behavior instead of reflecting reality.

People stop asking, “How do you feel?”<br>They start asking, “Did you complete it?”

The result is predictable.

Responses increase.<br>Authenticity decreases.

What looks like healthy participation is often just participation under duress.

## Annual Review Theater

The same dynamic shows up in performance reviews.

Especially in systems that mandate forced distributions or bell curves.

When leadership decides in advance that a certain percentage of people must underperform, the review process stops being diagnostic. It becomes distributive.

Someone has to land at the bottom — not because their work failed, but because the system requires contrast.

That decision doesn’t motivate improvement.<br>It redistributes anxiety.

Employees learn quickly that performance isn’t just about doing good work. It’s about positioning, perception, and survival.

## Rituals We Pretend Are Measurements

Over time, a quiet understanding forms.

Leaders tell themselves these systems surface insight.<br>Employees learn they’re rituals to be endured.

Surveys are something you get through.<br>Reviews are something you manage.

The organization feels informed.<br>The people feel managed.

Both sides play their role.

## A Personal Moment of Clarity

I once worked in an environment where leadership tracked engagement survey participation daily.

The goal was 85 percent.

Managers were sent lists of employees who hadn’t responded yet. Follow-ups were expected. Explanations were required.

At some point, the absurdity became hard to ignore.

If you have to threaten, pressure, or shame someone into taking a survey about how engaged they feel, you already have your answer.

## What These Metrics Really Reflect

Engagement surveys and annual reviews often function as mirror metrics.

They reflect the organization’s faith in measurement more than its understanding of people.

They tell us how much leaders trust the instruments they’ve built — not how much employees trust the system they’re inside.

And when metrics start measuring loyalty to the metric itself, something fundamental has been lost.

## A Quiet Question

Are your engagement and performance systems measuring connection… or compliance?
