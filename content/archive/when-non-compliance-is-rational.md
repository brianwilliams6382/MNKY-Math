## When Non-Compliance Is Rational

There comes a point where people stop following the signal.

Not because they’re careless. Not because they don’t understand it. Because following it no longer makes sense.

At first, this shows up quietly.

In some systems, it’s small.

Expense reporting becomes so detailed that people start approximating or delaying it… just to get their time back.

Calendars fill with meetings meant to improve alignment… so people begin declining them to actually get work done.

> Not to break the system. **To make the work possible.**

Nothing dramatic.

Just small adjustments.

But the pattern doesn’t stay small.

In other systems, it becomes harder to ignore.

Security teams face a constant stream of alerts — many of them low-confidence or false positives — so they begin filtering aggressively to focus on what might actually matter.

People receive repeated notifications about data breaches — important, serious signals — until they start ignoring them altogether.

> Not because the risk isn’t real. **Because the signal no longer feels meaningful.**

---

And in some environments —

The stakes are much higher.

In hospitals, patient monitoring systems generate continuous alerts.

Vital signs. Threshold changes. Potential issues.

But when alarms fire too often — especially when many aren’t actionable — staff begin to triage, delay, or silence them… just to function.

> Not because they don’t care. **Because they have to.**

The pattern is the same.

The system is still asking for something.

But the signal no longer reflects reality.

So people start choosing.

Not between right and wrong.

Between what the system says… **and what actually works.**

And when those two diverge far enough —

Compliance becomes a liability.

> Doing it “the right way” stops producing the right outcome.

---

So behavior shifts. Not away from the goal. Away from the signal.

Work still gets done.

Problems still get solved.

But it happens outside the system.

- Spreadsheets replace dashboards.
- Side conversations replace official channels.
- Experience replaces metrics.

> The system says one thing.<br>**_Reality says another. People follow reality._**

---

From the outside, this looks like non-compliance.

Lack of discipline. Resistance.

But from the inside — It’s adaptation.

Because the goal was never the metric. The goal was the outcome.

And when the metric stops pointing to the outcome…

Following it becomes irrational.

> So people don’t.

This is the moment most systems misread.

==They see deviation… and try to correct behavior.==

➡️ More rules.<br>➡️ More enforcement.<br>➡️ More oversight.

But enforcement doesn’t restore alignment.

It just forces people back into a system they no longer trust.

So one of two things happens.

People disengage.

Or they comply on the surface… and work around it underneath.

> The appearance of alignment increases. Actual alignment disappears.

This is where systems break.

Not when people stop caring.

When people start compensating.


## The reframe to hang with

If people are consistently working around the system…

It’s not a discipline problem.

> **It’s a signal problem.**

And when the signal no longer reflects reality…

Non-compliance isn’t failure.

It’s feedback.

➕

_What happens when the system corrects the behavior… instead of the cause?_
