# You Are Inside the System

As a Business Analyst, I used to think I was merely evaluating systems.

Looking at the numbers. Questioning decisions. Trying to make sense of what was happening.

It felt like I was standing outside of it.

Observing.

But over time, something became harder to ignore.

The system wasn’t just producing outcomes. It was shaping behavior.

Including mine.


## The Illusion of Standing Outside

Most of us believe we are interacting with systems from a distance.

We look at:

company performance metrics<br>workplace expectations<br>customer programs<br>policies and procedures

…and assume we are making independent decisions within them.

We evaluate. We react. We adjust.

It feels like agency.

But that framing hides something important.

> Systems are not passive environments.

➡️ Systems are active structures.

➡️ They guide behavior.


## A System You Already Understand

Think about driving on a highway.

When you’re the only car on a three-lane road, visibility is clear.

- Do you drive exactly the posted speed limit?
- Or just a little faster?

What lane do you choose?

Now add a few cars.

Traffic is light.

- If the car in front of you is moving faster, do you keep pace?
- If someone comes up behind you, do you adjust?

Now shift again.

Heavy traffic. Stop-and-go.

The road hasn’t changed. But your behavior has. Your tone probably has too.

Not because someone told you to change.

Because the system changed. And you adapted.

But the road itself isn’t the system.

- The other cars are part of it.
- The weather is part of it.
- The visibility, the timing, the flow.

The presence of a police car changes behavior instantly.

And you—

> you are part of that system too.

The way you drive becomes a signal for everyone around you.


## How Systems Actually Communicate

Every system communicates through signals.

Not through mission statements. Not through values posted on walls.

Through signals.

Things like:

- what gets measured
- what gets rewarded
- what gets reviewed
- what gets escalated
- what gets ignored

These signals don’t need to be explained.

They are learned.

Over time, people begin to understand:

- What matters
- What doesn’t
- What moves
- What stalls

And once that understanding forms, behavior begins to adjust.


## Adaptation Is Rational

This adjustment isn’t manipulation.

It’s adaptation.

- If a system rewards speed, people move faster.
- If it rewards volume, people produce more.
- If it rewards compliance, people follow process. (Or learn how to create the appearance of it.)

And unlike speed or volume, the appearance of following process can hold for a very long time. Not because people are trying to deceive. Because the system made that behavior useful.

Speed. Volume. Compliance.

In each case, adaptation happens. Not because people were told to.

Because it works. Because it is rational within the system.

And over time, this adaptation compounds.

-> What begins as a response becomes a pattern.

-> What becomes a pattern becomes expectation.


## When the System Moves Inside

At some point, something subtle shifts.

The system is no longer just influencing behavior.

It begins shaping identity.

People start to define:

- what good looks like
- what success means
- what responsible behavior is

…based on what the system signals.

And once that happens, the system is no longer external.

It has moved inside.


## The orientation to hang with

This is where most conversations about systems stop.

They focus on:

- improving metrics
- refining dashboards
- adjusting incentives

But before any of that matters, something more fundamental has to be understood.

You are not standing outside the system.

> You are inside it.

🔄 Shaped by it.

🔄 Responding to it.

🔄 And contributing to it at the same time.

And if that’s true, then the next question becomes harder to ignore:

What, exactly, is the system shaping you and others to become?
