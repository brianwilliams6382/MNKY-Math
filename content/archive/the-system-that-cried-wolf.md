## The System That Cried Wolf

A doorbell is a signal.

It means someone is there.

So you go to the door.

But sometimes… no one is there.

Just silence.

In the old days — before cameras, before notifications — you could get caught in a game you didn’t choose to play.

Kids called it ding-dong-ditch. They ring the bell. You walk to the door. No one there.

At first, you check every time. Then you hesitate.

Then maybe you wait a few seconds.

Then eventually — You stop getting up at all.

The signal is still there.

But it no longer means what it used to.

> Not because the bell stopped working. Because you stopped believing it.

---
The doorbell is harmless. Ignoring it doesn’t cost you much.

But not all signals are like that.

Some exist because something matters.

And in systems, those signals often look like metrics.

➡️ Dashboards.<br>➡️ Alerts.<br>➡️ Reports.

And just like the doorbell — They only work if you believe them.

But something else is happening.

Something slower. More dangerous.

> Once belief starts to erode… response follows.

You’re not just trusting the signal less. You’re starting to ignore it.

At first, it’s hesitation. You see the number… but you don’t move as quickly.

Then it becomes filtering.

You start deciding which signals matter… and which don’t.

Then eventually — It becomes silence.

The signal is still there.

But it no longer elicits action.

> Not because it’s wrong. Because it’s no longer believed.

---
This is the shift most systems don’t see.

They assume **==more signaling creates more awareness.==**

➡️ More dashboards.<br>➡️ More alerts.<br>➡️ More reporting.

But frequency doesn’t create clarity.

**_It creates desensitization._**

---
A signal that fires too often stops feeling urgent.

A signal that’s been wrong too many times stops feeling credible.

A signal that can be gamed stops feeling meaningful.

> And once a signal loses credibility… it loses its power.

Now the system has a new problem.

Not lack of data. Lack of response.

The signal is there.

But no one moves.

---
## The desensitization to hang with

A signal only works if it’s both seen and trusted.

Once it becomes predictable, manipulable, or constant…

It doesn’t disappear.

It fades into the background.

> **And when that happens… even real problems start to look like noise.**
