# When the Metric Replaces the Meaning

Metrics start as a way to understand what matters.

To track progress.<br>To evaluate outcomes.<br>To make better decisions.

They are a proxy for something real.


## But proxies don’t stay neutral for long.

The moment a metric becomes a target, behavior begins to shift around it. Not because people are trying to break the system. Because they are trying to succeed within it.

And then something more subtle happens. 

The metric stops measuring the thing. And starts becoming the thing.

➡️ Hitting the number becomes the goal.

Even if the underlying reality hasn’t improved.<br>
Even if it’s gotten worse.

> The system still sees success.

**Because the number says it is.**

---

This is where the distortion begins.

Not in intent.

In substitution.

<span class="mm-callout-emoji" style="--emoji: ' ';">**Customer satisfaction scores go up  →  But customer frustration remains.**</span>

<span class="mm-callout-emoji" style="--emoji: ' ';">**Sales numbers increase  →  But trust declines.**</span>

<span class="mm-callout-emoji" style="--emoji: ' ';">**Activity rises  →  But outcomes don’t.**</span>

The metric signals success. So the system believes everything is working as designed.

But what’s actually happening is something else.

**_==The system is becoming easier to satisfy than the reality it said mattered.==_**

And once that happens, **behavior follows**.

Not toward the outcome.

**Toward the number.**

---

This isn’t manipulation. It’s adaptation.

When a metric is what gets rewarded, it becomes what people optimize for.

And over time, the metric becomes easier to move than the thing it was meant to measure.

Effort shifts. Not toward improvement. Toward movement.

And movement starts to look like progress.

**==This is where systems become dangerous.==**

Not because they fail.

**Because they _appear_ to succeed.**

➡️ The numbers improve.<br>➡️ The dashboards look healthy.<br>➡️ The reports tell a consistent story.

But the underlying reality drifts further away.

And the wider that gap grows, the harder it becomes to see.

> Because the system can only see what it measures.<br>_And now it’s measuring the wrong thing._


At that point, correction becomes difficult.

Because the system no longer recognizes the problem.

It only recognizes the number.

And the number says everything is working.


## The signal to hang with

A metric is only useful as long as it points to something real.

When the number becomes the goal, the meaning begins to fade.

And once it fades far enough… the system can no longer tell the difference.
