# The Cost of Being Counted

It started as a number.<br>Then I began to change.

Steps… Calories… Streaks.

And then the daily check-in, the scale. A part of my morning routine. A single uncounted step that reveals another number — a lagging metric meant to shape what comes next.

Some days it changes what I eat.<br>Some days, just what I intend.<br>Some days… nothing at all.

**But it always registers.** Even when I try to ignore it.

The moment something begins to count you, it begins to shape you.

## What Gets Counted Becomes Visible

At some point, learning becomes scoring.

- Grades begin to stand in for understanding.
- Scores begin to stand in for ability.

Students learn what is seen and rewarded. Teachers are encouraged to teach what is measured.

The system begins to revolve around “making the grade.” And the grade becomes a badge — standing in for knowing.

What gets counted doesn’t just become visible.

It becomes the thing. The why.

## What Gets Evaluated Begins To Shift

We learn and adapt to what gets attention.

- A child notices what earns attention. 
- A social post reveals what earns engagement.

The response becomes the signal.

Over time, behavior adjusts. Not always intentionally. Not always consciously.

> But consistently.

Pavlov demonstrated this simply: what is reinforced begins to repeat.

At a biological level, this is how we learn.

- Rewards pull us forward.
- Avoidance steers us away.

Over time, these signals begin to wire behavior — often before we’re fully aware of it.

> The metric does its job.

And something else begins to take shape alongside it.

## When Counting Replaces Knowing

A sales leader sets activity targets.

The math works.

Calls lead to meetings.<br>Meetings lead to opportunities.<br>Opportunities lead to revenue.

But not every rep works the same way.

- One builds through relationships.
- Another is still learning the craft.

The number doesn’t see that.

Over time, both workers begin to change.

- One moves toward volume.
- The other toward effort.

The metric holds.

And something quieter shifts.

---

You start to notice it elsewhere.

**In healthcare:**

Recommendations expand.<br>Conversations repeat.<br>Eligibility grows.

_Your vaccination choices are being counted._

And it subtly changes how each interaction feels.

**In restaurants:**

Traffic slows. Margins tighten.<br>Portions adjust. Ingredients change. Staff thins.

Nothing is announced.

But everything feels different.

The customer doesn’t see the metric. They feel the result.

And they begin to change too.

---

At some point, the question shifts.

From: What matters?

> To: **What counts?**


## The Cost Of Being Counted

Being counted does not just track and score behavior.

It begins to define it.

You see it in performance reviews.

- What gets recognized becomes what gets repeated.
- What gets measured becomes what gets prioritized.

Over time, work aligns with visibility. **_Not always with value._**

You see it in parenting.

What earns attention begins to shape behavior.

Not always because it reflects who someone is. But because it reflects what is seen.

- Identity becomes performance.
- Direction becomes perception.
- Meaning becomes measurable.

The number starts as a reflection. Then becomes a reference point. Then a target. 

Then, eventually, a proxy for truth.

And once that happens, it becomes harder to tell the difference between:

> What is real — and — what is being measured

## A Familiar Pattern

You see this clearly in how organizations operate — because the pattern is easier to see at scale.

Before measurement, direction is often guided by human judgment and experience.

After measurement, direction fragments across metrics.

What was once one signal becomes many.

- Revenue
- Growth
- Efficiency
- Utilization

Each with its own owner. Each shaping its own behavior.

The system becomes coordinated through numbers — not always through meaning.

And the way people operate within it begins to change.

- How teams decide.
- How they prioritize.
- How they collaborate.

This shift becomes especially visible when a company moves from private to public. 

This doesn’t just raise capital. It introduces a new author — one that writes in numbers. That author is felt across every team. 

Because it’s not just the story that gets updated. The rules change too.

And this does not only happen to companies.

It happens anywhere something begins to count you.

At work.<br>Online.<br>In systems designed to measure performance.<br>In everyday interactions.

The structure changes.

And so do you.

## The pull to hang with

Awareness of what counts does not remove its influence.

➡️ Awareness makes the pressure visible.

➡️ Awareness reveals what is shaping your decisions.

➡️ Awareness clarifies whether what is being measured aligns with what actually matters.

Not everything that counts you should define you.

And not everything that defines you can be counted.

> Counting doesn’t just observe behavior. It helps create it.

Sometimes the shift begins by noticing where you’re being pulled… and whether the consequence is chosen — or quietly installed.

Then asking:

What is shaping you right now?

What do you count that shapes you?

What are you counting that shapes others?

What is counting you — without you choosing it?
