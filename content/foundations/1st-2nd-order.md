# First-order and second-order effects

Every system has a first-order effect: the thing it is meant to produce directly.

An engine produces force that can be converted into movement. That is the obvious output. It is why the engine exists.

But the engine also creates second-order effects. It requires fuel. Fuel requires access. Access requires infrastructure. Infrastructure changes land, labor, economics, geography, and eventually the environment.

Wind-powered systems were limited by where and when wind was available. Water-powered systems were more reliable, but tied production to rivers and often required dams or redirected water. Coal, wood, oil, and gas made engines more portable and scalable, but introduced extraction, depletion, pollution, and new dependencies.

The engine did not only create movement. It changed the systems around movement.

That is the MNKY Math concern: not only what a system is designed to do, but what else changes because it does it.
