## Patterned Susceptibility

**Patterned Susceptibility** is the idea that human beings are individually different but biologically similar enough that systems can reliably shape behavior through recurring pressures, rewards, defaults, [[def_signals|signals]], constraints, and [[def_friction|friction]].

Biology matters because systems do not shape behavior only through logic, rules, or conscious choice. They also interact with attention, stress, reward, anticipation, fatigue, [[def_threat-response|threat response]], comfort, belonging, and desire. 

A video game can create urgency or reward without physical danger. A movie can make people laugh, cry, tense up, hope, grieve, or remember something they have not experienced directly. The system works because it connects with human response.

Patterned susceptibility does not mean everyone responds the same way.

It means systems can rely on enough shared human tendencies to make certain responses more likely.

## MNKY Math usage

Patterned susceptibility helps explain why systems do not need perfect prediction to shape behavior.

They only need enough recurring human response to notice patterns, adjust conditions, and make certain behaviors more likely.

Sometimes a system measures human response so it can decide what to do next.

Sometimes it shapes human response so it can produce the outcome it wants.

This is why defaults, rankings, notifications, deadlines, rewards, friction, scarcity, social proof, and repeated signals can shape what people notice, avoid, repeat, protect, or pursue.

The MNKY Math question is not: **Will everyone respond the same way?**

The better question is: **_What response is this system making more likely often enough to matter?_**
