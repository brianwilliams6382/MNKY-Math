## Interior Systems

**Interior systems** are the biological, emotional, cognitive, and learned processes inside a person that shape attention, interpretation, behavior, and choice.

MNKY Math often examines external systems: workplaces, platforms, metrics, incentives, rules, tools, markets, and social conditions.

But behavior is not shaped by external systems alone.

**People also carry interior systems.**

These include memory, emotion, habit, stress response, fatigue, attention, identity, bodily signals, reward sensitivity, threat response, and the neurochemical processes that influence motivation, salience, comfort, avoidance, and desire.

Interior systems matter because they help explain why the same external condition may produce different responses in different people.

- A signal that feels motivating to one person may feel threatening to another.
- A choice that appears rational to one person may feel costly, unsafe, or invisible to someone else.
 - A preference that feels natural may have been shaped by repeated reward, avoidance, exposure, comfort, identity, or fear.

This does not mean biology explains everything.

👉 **It means biology is part of the system.**

MNKY Math includes interior systems because systems do not shape behavior only from the outside.

They also interact with what people have learned, felt, practiced, protected, avoided, and become.

The question is not only: What external system shaped this behavior?

The MNKY question is:

> What happened when the external system met the person’s interior system?
