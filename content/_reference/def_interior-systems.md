## Interior Systems

**Interior systems** are the biological, emotional, cognitive, and learned processes inside a person that shape attention, interpretation, behavior, and choice.

MNKY Math often examines external systems: workplaces, platforms, metrics, incentives, rules, tools, markets, and social conditions.

But behavior is not shaped by external systems alone.

**People also carry interior systems.**

These include memory, emotion, habit, stress response, needs, fatigue, attention, identity, bodily signals, reward sensitivity, threat response, learned response and the neurochemical processes that influence motivation, salience, comfort, avoidance, and desire.

In MNKY Math, Interior Systems matter because external systems do not act on neutral participants. This helps explain why the same external condition may produce different responses in different people.

- A signal that feels motivating to one person may feel threatening to another.
- A choice that appears rational to one person may feel costly, unsafe, or invisible to someone else.
 - A preference that feels natural may have been shaped by repeated reward, avoidance, exposure, comfort, identity, or fear.

This does not mean biology explains everything.

👉 **It means biology is part of the system.**

Interior Systems help us to explain why.

## In plain language

Interior Systems are the systems inside people that shape how they respond to the systems around them.

It is produced through the interaction between:

🔎 the system around the person<br>
🔎 and the system inside the person

This is why the same condition can produce different responses.

- One person may experience a rule as structure. Another may experience it as control.
- One person may experience a metric as guidance. Another may experience it as threat.
- One person may respond to pressure with compliance. Another may respond with resistance, avoidance, shutdown, or overperformance.

MNKY Math uses Interior Systems to avoid treating people as identical responders to external conditions. This is one reason [[def_patterned-susceptibility|patterned susceptibility]] matters: external systems can shape behavior because they interact with recurring human patterns of attention, stress, reward, fatigue, memory, threat response, and desire.

The question is not only: What external system shaped this behavior?

The MNKY question is:

> What happened when the external system met the person’s interior system?
