## Compulsion

**Compulsion is repeated behavior characterized by an increasing sense that it has to be performed, particularly when performing it conflicts with the person's broader intentions or goals.**

One influential cross-diagnostic definition identifies three central features: repetition, a felt sense that one _has to_ perform the behavior, and recognition that the behavior is not aligned with one's overall goal.

That makes **behavioral flexibility** particularly important.

The question becomes less:

> Do I do this frequently?

and increasingly:

> **How difficult is it for me not to do this?**

### Compulsion is not the same thing as habit

A [habit](def_habit) can be automatic while remaining easy to interrupt.

A compulsion can involve considerable conscious awareness and even active resistance.

Someone may know exactly what they are doing, wish not to do it, and still experience a strong pressure to continue or repeat it.

So:

> **Automatic does not necessarily mean compulsive.  
> Compulsive does not necessarily mean unconscious.**

### Compulsion can accompany routine, habit, and ritual

Compulsive behavior may become embedded inside a [routine](def_routine).

Repeated compulsive behavior may acquire habitual or automatic features.

A [ritual](def_ritual) may become compulsive when performing the sequence changes from something meaningful or preferred into something experienced as necessary and difficult not to perform.

But ritual itself is not compulsion.

### Compulsion is not addiction

Compulsive engagement can be part of [addiction](def_addiction), but compulsions occur outside addiction as well.

Compulsive behavior is also central to conditions such as obsessive-compulsive disorder, where repetitive behaviors or mental acts may be performed to reduce distress or prevent a feared outcome.

> **Compulsion describes a narrowing of behavioral flexibility.  
> It does not, by itself, define the larger condition producing that narrowing.**

### Related concepts

These concepts can overlap, but they describe different properties of repeated behavior rather than stages of a progression.

- [Routine](def_routine) describes **structure**.
- [Habit](def_habit) describes **learned cue-response tendency**.
- [Automaticity](def_automaticity) describes **how readily something occurs**.
- [Ritual](def_ritual) describes **meaning**.
- [Mastery](def_mastery) describes **developed capability**.
- [Compulsion](def_compulsion) describes **reduced behavioral flexibility**.
- [Addiction](def_addiction) describes **a broader clinical condition**.
