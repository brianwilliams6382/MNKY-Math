## Habit

**A habit is a learned tendency to respond in a familiar way when recurring cues or conditions appear, with less need for conscious deliberation.**

Habits develop as responses are repeatedly performed in recurring contexts. Over time, the context itself can increasingly help activate the response. In psychology, this cue-linked automaticity is a defining feature distinguishing habit from merely doing something frequently.

A person may initially decide:

> I'll put on my seatbelt.

Eventually, getting into the car may help produce the response without requiring the same deliberate decision.

### Habit is not the same thing as routine

A [routine](def_routine) is the recurring structure.

A habit is the learned response tendency.

A morning routine might include:

> wake → shower → dress → make coffee → leave

while individual actions inside that routine may become habitual to different degrees.

A habit also does not require a long sequence. A single response to a recurring cue can become habitual.

### Habit is not automaticity

[Automaticity](def_automaticity) is a **property** describing reduced reliance on conscious attention, effort, or intention.

Habit is one process through which automaticity commonly develops.

A behavior can become more automatic through extensive practice without necessarily fitting the everyday meaning of habit. Automaticity can therefore contribute to habit, routine, skill, and mastery.

### Habit is not compulsion or addiction

A habit may happen quickly or without much thought while still being relatively easy to interrupt.

A [compulsion](def_compulsion) involves something different: an increasing sense that the behavior _has to_ be performed, often despite conflict with broader intentions or goals.

An [addiction](def_addiction) is broader still and describes a clinically consequential disorder involving impaired control and continued engagement despite harm.

Habitual processes can participate in compulsive or addictive behavior.

But:

> **Habit does not automatically become compulsion.  
> Compulsion does not automatically become addiction.**

### Related concepts

These concepts can overlap, but they describe different properties of repeated behavior rather than stages of a progression.

- [Routine](def_routine) describes **structure**.
- [Habit](def_habit) describes **learned cue-response tendency**.
- [Automaticity](def_automaticity) describes **how readily something occurs**.
- [Ritual](def_ritual) describes **meaning**.
- [Mastery](def_mastery) describes **developed capability**.
- [Compulsion](def_compulsion) describes **reduced behavioral flexibility**.
- [Addiction](def_addiction) describes **a broader clinical condition**.
