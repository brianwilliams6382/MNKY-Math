## 4. The neurotransmitter arc needs special discipline

The neurotransmitter/system participation theme is high-potential, but also high-risk. It can become preachy, reductive, or pseudo-scientific if handled too casually.

The safer and stronger frame is not:

> Social media ruins dopamine.

It is closer to:

> The system is optimized for a high-frequency behavioral signal. Human neurobiology helps provide that signal. The loop is not a personal failure; it is a system-human alignment.

That framing fits MNKY Math because it keeps the article focused on **feedback loops**, not moral judgment. The idea of neurotransmitters as an “API” between human systems and digital systems is provocative and useful, but I’d probably soften the metaphor in final writing so it does not sound too tech-bro-ish. Something like:

> Neurotransmitters are not just internal chemistry. In digital environments, they become part of the interface between human attention and system optimization.

That feels more MNKY Math.
